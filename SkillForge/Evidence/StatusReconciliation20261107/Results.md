## What changed in this experiment

The prior broad map experiment exposed stale release recommendations and failed its efficiency rule. This attempt tested a smaller intervention: reconcile exact candidate identity and scope against current records before recommending integration, installation or replacement; distinguish retained, merged and installed; reuse already sufficient evidence. It adds no procedure or mandatory archive pass.

The candidate was frozen before its author inspected independently constructed cases. A separate reviewer qualified eight dossiers and five controls against exact source statements. Four development cases cover already integrated, genuinely pending, conflicting identity and unknown installed-version situations. Four different confirmation cases were reserved. They are constructed exercises, not new observations from real sessions. Matching prompts and source access, hidden reference judgments, exact input hashes and failure-aware reader accounting make this comparison more inspectable than regression counts alone.

## Decision and what remains unresolved

Both stages meet the local cost allowance (at most 125% baseline returned bytes and baseline +2 calls). Development has no required strict correctness gain. The historical follow-up advantage does not substitute for that missing development benefit or fresh confirmation, so the candidate fails adoption under the frozen rule. Preserve the narrower observation. Do not average opposite cost observations into a general advantage or interpret equal correct status judgments as equal overall quality.

The four reserved confirmation cases were **not run**: development did not establish a benefit to confirm. They remain preserved, with their reference answers withheld from the candidate author during this attempt. No wording revision, outcome-driven case tuning or repeated sampling was used to hunt for a winner.

We have not established that a missing status instruction causes the earlier mistakes. Evidence discovery, source packaging and run variability remain competing explanations. Another useful test needs naturally occurring status decisions with the input context captured before answering, including the records available and actually read. A predeclared set of such tasks could compare missed decisive evidence and wrong actions without manufacturing a weak baseline or restating obvious facts in compact dossiers. This is a next research condition, not an unexecuted requirement of this bounded experiment or a claim that the general problem is repaired.
