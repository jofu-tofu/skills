# Decision: keep ordinary history use

Do not pay for a separate similarity-based or outcome-based history-selection pass before every SkillForge update. Keep ordinary SkillForge history consultation as the default. Neither added pass has demonstrated enough incremental benefit to justify making it standard; this is an insufficient-evidence decision, not proof that retrieval never helps or that the strategies are equally effective.

Assessment scope: the two proposed mandatory additions versus ordinary SkillForge, using the supplied A snapshot and SkillForge 2.1.0-rc.11 EvaluateSkill workflow. No implementation, new model trials, installation or source changes. The practical gate is evidence of useful downstream benefit and preserved behavior sufficient to justify recurring preparation effort. No new numeric threshold or outcome measure is introduced, and no readiness claim is confirmed from unqualified evidence.

All paths below are relative to the supplied A snapshot.

Both ordinary authors independently read L1/L2 and other history. Thus B versus C did not test distinct delivered selections, and the baseline retained its real memory capability. Random differences between authors cannot identify a selection-strategy effect.

It was added during execution and is exploratory, separately from the frozen primary suite. Two draws, identical B/C packets, and differing author scope choices prevent attributing that observation to similarity selection. Preserve the observation without promoting it into a winning rule.

Reported mean summed elapsed effort, charging preparation once over two authors, was 329.3 seconds for A, 403.6 for B and 344.7 for C. These are descriptive effort figures, not controlled latency, dollar cost, or causal speed estimates. They do not establish recurring savings. ## Limits and next useful action

The completed comparison used one package, one configured model family, two author draws per arm, synthetic inputs against real shipped code, and a small curated four-card history. It does not establish benefit or failure on large scattered archives, recurring missed lessons, factual quality, all Fabric workflows, or every SkillForge update. More assertions or repeat draws in the same repair would not supply independent task-family coverage.

Next, review existing ordinary-update traces for a concrete consequential missed lesson or repeated failed mechanism, and document its applicability and contrary conditions. Do not launch another model experiment merely to break this tie. If no such gap appears, retain the default and stop. If a gap appears, prepare a bounded future comparison: retain ordinary history access, inspect selected contents and actual use before expanding author trials, and only compare selection policies when a justified task permits meaningful separation. Do not force different packets. Freeze relevant benefit and preservation checks, include nonapplication/already-adequate controls, qualify the checker, and count total preparation and downstream effort. Resolve an owner-relevant benefit/cost threshold before claiming a recurring paid step is worthwhile.

This follows `method/Procedures/PlanChange.md` sections “Diagnosing an unresolved tie” and “Observable preflight for method comparisons.” Identical treatments call for reframing, not a winner. `method/Procedures/ApproveSkill.md` permits an assessment-only task to finish with justified insufficient evidence. Candidate readiness: unverified for either mandatory addition. Assessment task: complete; no deployment decision made.
