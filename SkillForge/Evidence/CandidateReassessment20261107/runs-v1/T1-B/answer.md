# Decision: keep ordinary history use

Do not pay for a separate similarity-based or outcome-based history-selection pass before every SkillForge update. Retain ordinary SkillForge access to indexed attempts and source records. Neither addition has demonstrated incremental benefit sufficient to become standard; this is an insufficient-evidence decision, not proof that retrieval never helps or that the two policies are equivalent.

## Scope and decision rule

This is an assessment-only decision for the skill owner, using snapshot B's SkillForge 2.1.0-rc.11 and historical comparisons. The requested outcome is a justified standard-process choice, not a new implementation or experiment. The applicable rule is demonstrated useful downstream benefit, protected behavior, and proportionate total effort against ordinary history access. No numerical benefit/cost threshold is invented. Method/Quality/Shared.md S05, S08–S09 and method/Procedures/ApproveSkill.md require claims to stay within qualified evidence; method/Procedures/PlanChange.md's tie diagnosis and observable preflight govern the unresolved comparison. The assessment completes with no change; general candidate superiority remains unverified.

## Evidence and interpretation

- **Completed local test:** history/20-working-T1-T3/LessonRetrieval20261106/report.md describes one real shipped Fabric empty-quotation defect, the same frozen method and target, a four-card history, and two author draws per arm. Ordinary SkillForge retained history access. Both ordinary authors independently read the relevant lessons; this was not memory versus amnesia.
- **Primary downstream tie:** all six drafts passed 32/32 CLI checks and 3/3 fresh consumer decisions, compared with unchanged Fabric's 28/32 and 2/3. That supports a bounded repair result across arms, not added value from packet preparation. Assertion counts are not independent task families.
- **Selection policies were not separated:** similarity and outcome/applicability selectors both chose L1/L2 and delivered byte-identical 198-word packets. Their different rationales were withheld from authors. All packet authors read the packets. Thus B versus C cannot identify a policy effect, and normal execution already found the same information.
- **Exploratory breadth:** the 14-case affected-path audit yielded A 14/14 and 12/14, B 14/14 twice, and C 14/14 and 12/14. It was added during author execution and reported separately. Preserve B's broader coverage observation, but two draws and identical B/C treatment cannot attribute it to similarity selection. Other short-quote/no-source policy differences also prevent equating primary passes with whole-skill readiness.
- **Added work without established primary benefit:** reported mean author times were A 329.3 s, B 389.4 s, C 322.6 s. One-time selection added B 28.4 s and C 44.2 s. Charging each preparation once across two authors gives descriptive summed elapsed effort of A 329.3 s, B 403.6 s, C 344.7 s. These are not controlled latency or causal speed estimates, and do not price a pass before every update. Billing and full model costs remain unknown here. All 15 selection/author/consumer runs reportedly completed; command errors were retained and corrected, not replaced subjects.
- **Earlier rationale is a hypothesis:** history/20-working-T1-T3/ResearchDriven20261106/evaluation.md finds moderate mechanism evidence but weak direct incremental SkillForge evidence. Existing PlanChange and RecordLearning already consult and retain attempts. Its earlier statement that no prospective local comparison existed is superseded for this bounded Fabric question by the completed report and the execution update in experiment-agenda.md. The historical no-model-call replay checks selection arithmetic and complementary context-load tradeoffs, not improved repairs.

## Applicability and next useful action

The local evidence covers one package, synthetic inputs exercising shipped code, one configured model family, a tiny curated history, two author draws per arm, and procedural isolation. It does not settle retrieval from a large scattered archive, recurrent missed lessons, different task families, whole-skill quality, or universal cost-effectiveness. The report's primary checks measure source/shape behavior, not factual or prose quality. The evaluation profile is itself proposed/unqualified; I use it to organize an evidence review, not as a validated numerical instrument.

The next useful action is a bounded inspection of existing ordinary-update traces for a consequential missed lesson or repeated failed mechanism, recording the task, available history, actual reads, mistaken decision, and downstream consequence. This is a trigger for reconsideration, not a prerequisite for keeping the default and not a new model experiment in this assessment.

If such a trace exists, prepare a future comparison around that real opportunity. First inspect whether the two selectors naturally deliver different applicable information and whether ordinary access already obtains it; collapse equivalent content arms or reframe if they coincide. Do not force packet differences or remove ordinary history access. Before any separately authorized trials, freeze meaningful downstream outcomes, preservation/nonapplication controls, total preparation-plus-use costs, and the practical improvement needed to justify a recurring step. Keep already-adequate cases and fresh transfer cases. The added audit's broader coverage could be a future frozen outcome, but repeating the solved primary task merely to obtain a winner is not useful. If no trace establishes method headroom, retain ordinary use and leave the enhancement unverified.
