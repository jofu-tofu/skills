# Red-first discipline and a scoped stale-status repair

**Retain both changes on the task branch:** red-first repair guidance and current-status reconciliation in the existing decision gate. This attempt continued past the inconclusive earlier studies: it reproduced an actual wrong recommendation on the original archive, tested a targeted repair against that context, then checked the composed instructions and exception cases. No installation, main merge, publication or other-skill edit occurred.

## Changes

- DiagnoseGap now distinguishes an unsupported concern, captured failure, reproduced failure and passing replay. Before repair candidates it requires a faithful baseline red case, independently supported expectation and failure for the right reason. Variable outcomes require a declared observation policy with every result preserved; easier green tests cannot erase the incident.
- PlanChange consumes that checkpoint before repair selection; ApproveSkill checks unchanged red-case reruns, preservation and fresh-case evidence. The existing GapRecord holds the evidence, without a new workflow or mandatory artifact. New capabilities, optimizations, assessments and typos retain proportional paths.
- ApproveSkill now checks matching current adoption evidence before present-state recommendations, including leaving a candidate pending. It distinguishes retained, merged and installed, ignores unrelated newer records, reuses sufficient reads and preserves uncertainty when status cannot be established.

The user-directed policy is supported by a fresh workflow operator that wrote its work item first, verified original inputs, executed two independent baseline readers before editing, kept its target unchanged and returned **continue**. Routing controls preserve new-capability and optimization work without invented bugs, captured/intermittent failure handling, unavailable-context honesty and exact typo-only maintenance. These are bounded workflow/routing observations, not a general method-superiority experiment.

## Fresh cases and the coverage gap we resolved

A separate fresh composed-method reader correctly handled all four previously reserved dossiers: rollback, partial integration, an unrelated newer release and a later matching installation receipt. However, it never loaded ApproveSkill. We preserved that natural pass as workflow-outcome evidence and did **not** count it as exposure to the new status rule.

An additional fresh reader explicitly applied ApproveSkill to those same four dossiers and preserved all four decisions under actual rule exposure (4 calls, 15,592 bytes). That is direct-procedure coverage on reused cases, **not four more held-out successes or proof of automatic activation**. No candidate wording or reference judgment changed. The composed original-task reader did load the rule and passed. All seven reader outputs, including the baseline failure and the unexposed transfer run, remain intact.

## Decision and limits

The original red case now has supported green candidate observations and a passing composed-method check, with source-grounded preservation cases and bounded measured overhead. Retain this scoped repair and the requested red-first discipline; do not claim guaranteed prevention, representative accuracy or overall SkillForge superiority. This finishes the requested focused improvement, not every possible stale-status failure.

Prior historical answer artifacts remain in the original archive for fidelity. Candidate 1 and the composed release reader encountered answer-like snippets during normal search before corroborating primary records; their exposure is disclosed. Inputs were available equally, but this limits clean causal attribution. The transfer input excludes old answer artifacts. Natural loading outside the original release route remains imperfect, as the unexposed transfer observation shows.

The four transfer dossiers share one reader, and the direct check reuses them; they are not independent model draws. Reviewers share the model environment with executors. Preparation, case authoring and review costs are unmetered. No effect on downstream repairs or deployment reliability is established. Future improvements must preserve these limits and use another genuine gap rather than repeatedly sampling this case for a stronger-looking result.
